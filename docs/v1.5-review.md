### 0.1 What is after the first references marker? (ICLR, failure mode "post-reference text is not appendix")

`python3 scripts/review_v15_boundary.py --years 2017 2018 2020 2023 2024 2025 2026 2027 --sample 500`
(seed 15; 500 random papers per year, all 486 in 2017) → `results/review/boundary_iclr.csv`.

| | 2017 | 2018 | 2020 | 2023 | 2024 | 2025 | 2026 | 2027 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| median share after first marker | .075 | .109 | .193 | .266 | .277 | .328 | .384 | .473 |
| text between first and last marker, share of post-ref chars | .026 | .042 | .009 | .009 | .008 | .004 | .012 | .015 |
| bibliography-like lines, share of post-ref chars | .008 | .006 | .007 | .009 | .010 | .012 | .012 | .004 |
| first heading after refs is an appendix heading | .61 | .67 | .81 | .76 | .74 | .70 | .76 | .65 |
| … is a statement (ethics, reproducibility, AI use) | 0 | 0 | .002 | 0 | 0 | .006 | .028 | .024 |
| no text after refs | .33 | .28 | .12 | .16 | .17 | .08 | .05 | .05 |
| two or more separate reference blocks | .008 | .008 | .002 | .006 | .002 | .002 | .006 | .014 |
| page of first references marker (median) | 9 | 10 | 9 | 10 | 10 | 11 | 10 | 10 |
| pages (median) | 13 | 14 | 16 | 18 | 19 | 22 | 23 | 24 |

Reading: the 7.4% → 47.2% medians reconstruct from source records (sample medians .075 and .473). For ICLR,
post-reference text is overwhelmingly appendix: reference residue (unomitted bibliography lines) is about
1% of post-reference characters in every year, text between reference-page markers 1–4%, and
post-reference statement sections appear in 2–3% of 2026–27 papers. The remaining "other" first
headings (6% in 2017, 28% in 2027) were inspected on a 1-in-20 sample of 2027: mostly appendix
headings not preceded by a letter (`A.1`, `RELATED WORK`, `CONTENTS`, `PROOFS`, `EXTENDED RELATED WORK`),
plus a handful of bibliography entries that escaped the omission step. The references marker sits at a
near-constant page (9–11), consistent with a fixed main-body page limit while the total grows.

Reviewer correction (own check): a first version of the "appendix placed before references" flag
matched any wrapped body line beginning with "Appendix B …" and reported 22% for 2027. Restricted to
whole heading lines it is 0–0.6% in every year. Logged here so the inflated figure is not reused.

### 0.2 Other venues: the post-reference span contains checklists (failure mode "post-reference text is not appendix")

`python3 scripts/review_v15_boundary.py --venue neurips --years 2022 2024 --sample 500` and
`python3 scripts/review_v15_checklist.py` (all papers) → `results/review/checklist_share.csv`.

| venue-year | median post-ref share | first post-ref heading | note |
|---|---|---|---|
| NeurIPS 2022 | .067 | checklist 70% | Post-ref text is mostly the paper checklist (78% of papers; ~71% of post-ref chars on average). Excluding it, median post-ref share is 0.2%: no appendix in the PDF (supplementary was a separate file). |
| NeurIPS 2024 | .385 | appendix 88% | 99.8% carry the checklist after the appendix ("Answer: [Yes] / Justification:"); it is 16% of post-ref chars (median; mean 23%). Excluding it, median post-ref share falls from .38 to .31. |
| TMLR 2024, COLM 2025, ICLR 2027 | .25 / .37 / .47 | — | no checklist after refs (≤0.1%). |

Implication for Lane A: any NeurIPS (and any other checklist venue) appendix measure must remove the
checklist first, or NeurIPS 2022 → 2024 growth is overstated at the low end and the NeurIPS level is
inflated by ~16% of post-ref text.

### 0.4 Is the growth an extraction artifact? (failure mode "PDF extraction changes masquerading as growth")

`python3 scripts/review_v15_extraction.py` (ICLR, all papers; 2027 every 6th batch, n = 7,163) →
`results/review/extraction_pages_iclr.csv`. Pages come from the raw PDF text layer (`direct` config), joined on
`pdf_sha256`; page positions of the reference markers come from form feeds in the omissions text.

| | 2017 | 2020 | 2023 | 2025 | 2026 | 2027 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| omissions page count = PDF page count | 99.4% | 99.7% | 99.8% | 99.7% | 99.5% | 99.8% |
| PDF pages (median) | 13 | 16 | 19 | 22 | 23 | 24 |
| page of first references marker | 9 | 9 | 10 | 11 | 10 | 10 |
| reference-list pages | 2 | 3 | 3 | 4 | 4 | 3 |
| pages after the last references marker: P25 / P50 / P75 / P90 | 0/1/4/7 | 2/4/8/13 | 3/6/11/18 | 4/8/14/22 | 5/10/16/24 | 7/12/19/30 |
| main-body chars (median) | 30.2k | 30.6k | 33.1k | 35.5k | 33.4k | 31.9k |
| main-body chars per page | 3,144 | 3,261 | 3,329 | 3,293 | 3,231 | 3,188 |
| omissions/clean char ratio | .83 | .80 | .77 | .76 | .76 | .78 |
| tables omitted after refs, per paper | 1.9 | 4.6 | 7.5 | 9.0 | 10.6 | 15.3 |

Reading: the growth is in PDF pages, not just extracted characters, so it is not an extraction artifact.
Main-body length is roughly flat (30–36k chars, 9–11 pages, constant characters per page); reference lists add
1–2 pages; pages after the references move across the whole distribution (P25 0 → 7, P90 7 → 30), not only in
a tail. Two measurement cautions for Lane A: tables omitted from appendices rise eightfold, so appendix
*characters and tokens* undercount appendix *pages* more in later years; and the `omissions/clean` ratio moves
by 5 points, so character comparisons across years should be stated in the omissions config explicitly.

### 0.3 ICLR 2027 AI-use statements: extraction and leakage into the body (failure modes "template wording", "leakage")

`python3 scripts/review_v15_disclosures.py` (all 42,344 papers) → `results/review/disclosure_audit_2027.csv`,
`results/review/disclosure_heading_variants_2027.csv` (heading lines only).

| measure | value |
|---|---|
| papers with an AI-use-like heading (broad net) | 96.0% |
| papers whose AI heading the v2 remover (`sections.py`) matches | 94.0% |
| AI heading present but v2 matches none (statement stays in the body) | 1.9% |
| papers with a template signature sentence left in the cleaned main body | 1.9% (3.4% of papers that have any signature) |
| statement placed after the references (outside main body anyway) | 4.6% |
| cut section that hits the 5,000-char cap / overruns into figures or tables | 0.08% / 0.25% |
| median / P90 statement length (chars) | 537 / 1,168 |

Unmatched heading variants include "use of AI assistance", "disclosure of AI use", "AI disclosure
statement", "artificial intelligence (AI) use statement", "statement of AI use", "declaration of LLM
usage", and run-in headings ("AI Use Statement: In this work, …" on one line).

The 2027 template, reconstructed from a sentence census of 5,079 cut sections (1-in-8 sample), is a
fill-in with **both polarities over the same task list**: "In this work, we used generative AI tools to
⟨tasks⟩. We have not used generative AI tools to ⟨tasks⟩. Additionally, we used generative AI tools to
⟨other uses⟩. We have reviewed all AI-assisted work. We take responsibility for the final content of this
work, including text, claims or artifacts produced with the aid of generative AI." The task list
("generate synthetic data sets, help develop theoretical models or conceptual frameworks, formulate
mathematical claims, provide critical ingredients for proving mathematical claims, assist in the writing of
proofs, propose or refine hypotheses, design or provide feedback on research methodology or experiments,
implement methods, assist with translation, clean and reformat datasets, support qualitative and thematic
data analysis, interpret results") appears verbatim in the negated clause of many statements.

Consequences the reviewer will check in Lanes B/C:
1. Phrase frequencies of task names are polarity-mixed; a frequency table must split affirmed and negated
   clauses or it will rank negated template items as common uses.
2. Prose editing / readability is not in the required-disclosure task list; it tends to appear only in the
   optional "Additionally, …" sentence. "Explicit non-use" of the required tasks is therefore **not** a
   statement that no writing help was used, and "no AI use disclosed" ≠ "no AI used".
3. The 1.9% of papers whose statement survives in the body inflate any disclosure/body phrase-overlap
   measure for exactly those papers. Lane C must use a broader remover or exclude them, and show the
   overlap result with and without them.

## 1. Checklist for each gate

Lane A (Gate 1)
- [ ] Reconstruct per-year main-body and post-ref medians/percentiles from source for two years.
- [ ] Hand-check ≥10 papers' boundaries per cohort (early/late) against the raw text.
- [ ] Check NeurIPS checklist removal, NeurIPS 2021–22 separate supplementary, TMLR (no page limit).
- [x] (baseline in 0.4) Extraction change: compare `direct` page counts and characters with `omissions`; omission-marker
      density by year (tables/equations dropped from appendices undercount appendix characters).
- [ ] Version mix: growth within under-review submissions only.
- [ ] Concentration: share of total appendix growth from the top decile of papers.
- [ ] Page-limit / supplementary-rule claims cite a source.

Lane B (Gates 2–3)
- [ ] Extraction coverage vs the 96.0% / 94.0% baseline; how run-in and variant headings are handled.
- [ ] Template vs author text: how template sentences are identified; polarity split for task phrases.
- [ ] Hand labels: sample size ≥150, stratification, who labelled, and that labels were fixed before any
      classifier was scored. Held-out set not used for prompt tuning.
- [ ] Per-class precision/recall and confusion matrix; abstention rate; negation cases specifically.
- [ ] Re-label 30 statements blind and compare.

Lane C (Gate 4)
- [ ] Body text excludes all disclosure text (including the 1.9% leaked cases) and other statements.
- [ ] Shuffled-pairing negative control matched on topic and length; class sizes and CIs reported.
- [ ] Multiple-comparison correction declared before the scan; preregistered feature list fixed in a
      commit that precedes the results commit.
- [ ] Associations survive dropping highly formulaic statements.
